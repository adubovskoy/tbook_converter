# GOLD-lite: an absolute anchor for En→Ru, En→De and En→Fr

Plan item **A3**. Date 2026-09-19. Producer at `a7f61cf87c9f-dirty` (the working tree carried the
in-flight lexcheck/langcheck work of A4; every arm records the commit in `manifest.meta`).

Before today the workspace had **no gold or human reference for any pair** (EVIDENCE §6.3): every
quality claim on record was relative (arm A vs arm B, judged blind) or structural (coverage, script,
dictionary plausibility). GOLD-lite was planned in July and never built. This report is the first
absolute anchor, and it is an honest one: **0 sentences are gold, 756 are silver, 144 are disputed**,
and the weak point — no human reader for German or French — is recorded as such rather than papered
over.

As a by-product the three conversions below are the **strong-model reference arms A2 will use as its
upper anchor**.

Notes on the numbers, so none of them is read for more than it is:

- **The one error per run is expected and benign.** Each arm shows exactly one `400` in the
  `glossary` phase: `google/gemini-3.7-flash` rejects `reasoning:{enabled:false}`, and the client
  then latches `effort:"minimal"` for the rest of the run — the shipped fix of
  `four-model-bench-2026-08.md` §5.1, confirmed working three times here.
- **Wall time was measured under CPU contention.** An unrelated align-knob grid (D4) was running on
  the same machine; load average sat at 18–26 on 22 cores throughout. The local embedding aligner is
  3m07s–3m40s of each arm's wall time, so an uncontended run is faster. The **API** numbers (cost,
  request counts, translate throughput) are unaffected.
- **The fr translate phase shows `trunc=4`** — four responses stopped for a non-`stop` reason and
  were retried; the arm still finished with 0 empty translations.
- **langcheck's single German flag is a false positive:** the source is
  `“Is Briony Lodge, Serpentine Avenue, St. John’s Wood.”`, a sentence of proper nouns only, which
  is correctly left almost unchanged. Residual leak across all three arms is effectively **0.0 %** —
  no Cyrillic in the Latin targets, which is what the per-target glossary namespaces buy (EVIDENCE
  §3.B).
- **lexcheck and tapledger are structural numbers** and are never summed with the judged counts in
  §4 (rule of evidence 2). The tap-ledger shape reproduces the panel's `sample.tbook` ranking almost
  exactly — de cleanest, fr next, ru most shared — on a different producer and a different model,
  which is a small independent confirmation that the ranking is a property of the language pair, not
  of one book.
- **German gets no gender tag**: `de` is absent from `genderMarkingTargets`, visible above as
  "0 gendered" where ru has 32 and fr 30. That is B2's open question, not a defect of this run.

## 3. The GOLD-lite sets and the stride rule

The same **300 source sentences** were drawn once and reused for all three pairs, so the sets are
directly comparable per sentence.

**Walk order** — exactly the order the converter segments the book: manifest chapter order →
paragraphs in order → sentences in order. Table cells, figure captions and footnote bodies are
separate chapter members and are **not walked**, which is how the 25 contents-table cells are
excluded without a special case.

**Candidate rule**, applied to the 1 642 prose sentences in that order:

| filter | dropped |
|---|---|
| fewer than 4 source words | 191 |
| empty translation in any of the three arms | 0 |
| duplicate source (keep the first occurrence) | 1 |
| **candidates** | **1 450** |

**Stride**: for `k = 0 … 299`, pick `candidates[floor(k × 1450 / 300)]` — an even stride of
**4.833**, no randomness, no seed, reproducible by re-running `gold_sample.py`. Chapter spread of the
300: ch1 1 (the book title), ch2 118, ch3 107, ch4 74.

Each line of `bench-quality/gold/en-<tgt>.jsonl` is
`{id, chapter, para, sent, src, ref, ref_model, status}` plus `check` and `check_reason` after §4;
`id` (`h4-001` … `h4-300`) denotes the same source sentence in all three files.

## 4. Adversarial check by a second family

The reference is `google/gemini-3.7-flash`; the checker is **`claude -p --model sonnet`** through the
`claude` CLI — a different model family, run with the same rate discipline as `judge_pairs.py`:
batches of 10, `--jobs 5`, 30 s backoff, one fresh process per batch, re-runnable to fill gaps.
900 pairs, 90 batches, 0 failures. The checker sees the English source and one translation, with no
wider context, and returns `ok | minor | wrong` plus a one-line English reason quoting the offending
words verbatim.

| pair | ok | minor | wrong | → silver | → disputed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **en-ru** | 243 (81.0 %) | 49 (16.3 %) | **8 (2.7 %)** | 243 | 57 |
| **en-de** | 254 (84.7 %) | 40 (13.3 %) | **6 (2.0 %)** | 254 | 46 |
| **en-fr** | 259 (86.3 %) | 38 (12.7 %) | **3 (1.0 %)** | 259 | 41 |

Across the 300 source sentences: **187 are `ok` in all three pairs**, 89 are disputed in exactly one,
17 in two, and **7 in all three** (`h4-062`, `h4-082`, `h4-083`, `h4-179`, `h4-183`, `h4-230`,
`h4-291`). Two sentences are called `wrong` in two different pairs. A sentence disputed in all three
targets is evidence about the **source** — an ambiguity or a pun every language stumbles on — not
about any one target language, and those are the first the human pass should look at.

### 4.1 Three `wrong` examples per pair, verbatim

**en-ru**

| src | ref | checker |
|---|---|---|
| `How did your mother take the matter?”` | `«Ещё один вопрос.` | «Ещё один вопрос» invents unrelated content, dropping the actual question «How did your mother take the matter?» |
| `Jump, Archie, jump, and I’ll swing for it!”` | `Прыгай, Арчи, прыгай, а я возьму вину на себя!` | «swing for it» (be hanged) rendered as «возьму вину на себя» (take the blame), losing the fatal stakes |
| `“I will not look.”` | `«Я не буду искать».` | «искать» (search) mistranslates 'look' (watch/observe), changing the meaning |

The first of those is a **substitution**: the model returned a different sentence altogether. Verified
directly in the file (`chapters/ch4.json`, paragraph 64, sentence 1). Nothing the pipeline ships can
see this — validation, coverage, `q`, lexcheck and langcheck all pass it, exactly as EVIDENCE §3.D
predicts.

**en-de**

| src | ref | checker |
|---|---|---|
| `The crudest of writers could invent nothing more crude.”` | `Der stümperhafteste aller Schriftsteller könnte nichts Plumpers erfinden.“` | «nichts Plumpers» has a bogus ending; comparative nominalized after nichts must be «nichts Plumperes» |
| `There is only one point on which I must insist.` | `Es gibt nur einen Punkt, auf den ich bestehen muss.` | «bestehen auf den» — «bestehen auf» governs Dativ, not Akkusativ; should be «auf dem» |
| `“I then lounged down the street and found … that there was a mews in a lane …` | `„Ich schlenderte dann die Straße hinunter und stellte fest … dass sich in einer Gasse … Mews befanden.` | «Mews» left untranslated as an English word standing in as a German plural noun |

These are the three classes the plan predicted for German — **non-existent word**, **case
government**, **untranslated term** — found on the strongest model the repo has measured, with no
repair pass. A fourth, `«von Pferdehufe»` for dative plural `Pferdehufen`, is agreement.

**en-fr**

| src | ref | checker |
|---|---|---|
| `It hadn’t pulled up before she shot out of the hall door and into it.` | `Elle ne s'était pas encore arrêtée qu'elle jaillit de la porte du vestibule et monta dedans.` | «Elle ne s'était pas encore arrêtée» wrongly makes the woman the subject of stopping, when the source «It hadn't pulled up» refers to the cab |
| `“He’s a brave fellow,” said a woman.` | `« C'est un brave homme », dit une femme.` | «un brave homme» (adjective before noun) means a good/kind man, not a courageous one as in «brave fellow» |
| `“Oh, yes, mother is alive and well.` | `« Oh, oui, mère est vivante et bien portante.` | «mère est vivante» drops the required possessive: standard French needs «ma mère», not a bare noun as subject |

French produced the fewest `wrong` verdicts of the three, and its three are subtler: a referent
error, the classic `brave` pre/post-nominal sense flip, and a missing possessive.

## 5. Status, and what these sets may and may not be used for

**0 gold · 756 silver · 144 disputed**, i.e. per pair:

| pair | gold | silver | disputed |
|---|---|---|---|
| en-ru | **0** | 243 | 57 |
| en-de | **0** | 254 | 46 |
| en-fr | **0** | 259 | 41 |

Nothing is `gold` yet, by construction: `gold` is reserved for a sentence a human has read and
either confirmed or corrected. `bench-quality/gold/en-ru-review.md` is that pass waiting to happen —
300 numbered src/ref pairs with the checker's verdict and reason, **disputed first**, and
instructions to set `status: "gold"` (correcting `ref` and setting `ref_model: "human"` where
needed). The author reads Russian, so en-ru is the one set that can reach gold.

`en-de-review.md` and `en-fr-review.md` have the same shape, and their headers say plainly that
**no one on the team reads German or French**, so nothing in them is promoted: those two sets stay
**silver** as a whole, on one machine checker's word.

The plan's kill criterion for A3 — *"if two independent verifiers disagree on > 15 % of sentences,
the set is not gold and is renamed silver"* — **cannot be evaluated**: there is one verifier, not
two, and no human. The sets are therefore silver by construction rather than by a failed test. That
is the honest reading and it is the same weak point the plan itself flagged under "De/Fr readers".

**May be used for**

- An **absolute regression alarm**: re-translate these 300 sentences with a changed pipeline, re-run
  `gold_check.py`, and compare the `wrong` + `minor` counts against the baseline above. A jump is a
  signal worth chasing.
- The **A2 upper anchor**: these three `.tbook`s are the strong-model arm, produced at HEAD with the
  run record in `manifest.meta`.
- A **source-difficulty probe**: the 7 sentences disputed in all three pairs, and the 2 called
  `wrong` in two pairs.
- A worked list of real defect examples per pair, for D2's target-specific prompt blocks.

**May not be used for**

- **Prompt tuning of any kind.** Fitting a prompt to these 300 sentences destroys the only absolute
  anchor the workspace has. This is the rule the plan states and it is not negotiable.
- **A ground truth.** `ref` is one model's output that a second model mostly did not object to. On
  its own showing it contains at least 17 defects across the three pairs, one of them an outright
  substitution.
- **A BLEU/COMET-style score against the pipeline's own output.** The reference and the arms under
  test come from related systems; a similarity score would measure family resemblance.
- **A relative claim.** Nothing here is a blind pairwise comparison; rule of evidence 1 (≥ 400 pairs,
  both orders) is untouched by this work.
- **Cross-pair quality comparison.** That en-fr shows 3 `wrong` and en-ru 8 says as much about how
  strictly one checker judges three languages as about the translations. A checker calibration —
  A4's job — has to come first.

## 6. What is left undone

1. **The human Russian pass.** `en-ru-review.md` exists; nobody has read it. Until then en-ru has
   0 gold sentences like the others.
2. **A second independent verifier**, so A3's own kill criterion becomes measurable. A second strong
   model in a different family, run on the same 900 pairs, would cost one more checker sweep and
   would turn "silver by construction" into "silver, measured".
3. **Checker calibration.** The checker's own precision is unknown: how often does `wrong` name a
   real defect, and how much does it miss? A4 calibrates the free gates; the same 300-sentence
   scaffolding would calibrate this checker.

   are not one author. These sets are Conan Doyle only.
